ORDINARY LEMMAS AND FINITE-COVER SOUNDNESS

SCOPE
These notes give the numbered lemmas and conditional finite-cover soundness
theorem. The theorem requires complete domains and a contradiction at every
terminal leaf. It is distinct from verifying an implementation and executing
a complete cover. The accompanying manuscript supplies the final exposition
and correspondence qualifications. Original rule numbering is retained.

The only external theorem used within the shadow arguments is classical
Macaulay growth for an actual standard total-degree graded quotient over Q.
Its exact source statement is a published leaf: Eliahou, arXiv:1703.01761v1,
Definitions5.1/5.2, Notation5.4, Theorem5.5; the all-positive binomial convention
is also stated in Gao, arXiv:2512.21590v1, Section1/Theorem1.1. The quotient
hypotheses and the numerical adapters are proved here, not imported.
The following optional Wilf dispatch leaves are distinct from these proofs:
full embedding dimension e<=3 (Fröberg--Gottlieb--Häggkvist, On numerical
semigroups, Semigroup Forum35,63--83, DOI10.1007/BF02573091; exact inspected
corroboration Delgado--Eliahou--Fromentin2310.07742v1, Theorem1.1(1)); q<=3
(Eliahou1703.01761v1, Corollary6.5 at q=3, Proposition3.13 at q=2,
and q=1 directly); early-empty q>=4 with every
alpha_i=0 for i<ceil(q/2) (Eliahou--Marín-Aragón2006.01480v1,
Theorem3.1 and Proposition2.7); and the proof-level e,q>=4, n<=3q-1
consequence of Eliahou1909.03699v1, Section5. Its adapter uses depth
tau(X)=n-q, not the graph's vertex cardinality; it is not obtained by removing
the hypothesis of the paper's numbered ratio theorem. Exact primary-source
adapters and inspection limits are supplied separately in
IMPORTS_v1.txt/SOURCES_v1.json: I1 small e, I2 small q, I3 early-empty and

conductor200, I8 genus100, I9 citation-only predecessor. In I3 the
right-primitive transfer identity is W=W0+|P intersect[c,c+m)|(n-q).

The certificate soundness theorem can simply retain every q=1,...,n and use
none of those optional gates. For a conductor cutoff H, the theorem accepts
an independently proved or explicitly admitted Wilf leaf for c<=H. The
computation uses as admitted results Bacher2604.25051v8, Section2.3, c<=200 INCLUSIVE,

CONVENTIONS
A numerical semigroup is a cofinite additive submonoid S of N containing0.
Its conductor is c=min{a in N:[a,infinity) subset S}; its multiplicity is
m=min(S\{0}); its primitive elements are positive elements not expressible
as a sum of two positive S-elements; e counts ALL primitives. Put
n=|S intersect[0,c)|, including0, and W=ne-c. For S=N, c=n=0 and W=0;
all remaining definitions concern S!=N, so c>0,m>=2. Set
q=ceil(c/m), rho=qm-c, A={a in S:a-m notin S},
lambda(x)=floor((x+rho)/m), A_i={a in A:lambda(a)=i}.
For 1<=i<q put alpha_i=|A_i|. Let D be the positive decomposable elements
of A, delta=|D|, R=A intersect(0,c) intersect D, R_i=R intersect A_i,
kappa_i=|R_i|, beta_i=alpha_i-kappa_i,
T=A intersect(0,c), B=T\D, s=|B|=sum beta_i, K=|R|=sum kappa_i.
Use [a,b]_Z={z in Z:a<=z<=b}, empty for a>b. A strict interval upper
bound b means inclusive integer endpoint b-1. All monomials permit repeated
variables; all pattern and divisor statements include their multiplicities.
Empty sums are0, empty products1, and binom(a,b)=0 for integers0<=a<b.
Our finite D means D_A=A intersect((S\{0})+(S\{0})). The source notation
D in IMPORTS_v1 instead means the infinite set D_S=(S\{0})+(S\{0});
source |X intersect D_S| equals our delta, never |D_S|.

1. APERY PARTITION, FULL EMBEDDING DIMENSION, AND UNIVERSAL SUBSUMS.
There is exactly one A-element in each residue modulo m; A subset[0,c+m),
A_0={0}, and every positive A-element has level1,...,q. Its primitive
elements are exactly the primitives of S other than m. Every decomposable
A-element is a sum of members of B. Every positive partial sum of ANY
representation of an A-element as positive S-elements belongs to A; every
proper positive partial sum is <c. In particular all proper primitive
submonomials of such a representation are left Apéry values. Moreover
 m=e+delta,
 n=q+sum_(i=1)^(q-1)(q-i)alpha_i,
 c=q(e+delta)-rho,
 W<0 iff qdelta-rho>(n-q)e.                              (1.1)
One has 1<=q<=n, 0<=rho<m, kappa_1=0, and e>=1+s. In fact e>=2.

Proof. Cofiniteness gives a least S-element in each residue class. It is
exactly the one whose predecessor by m is outside S. Every S-element has
a unique form a+tm with a in A,t>=0. If a>=c+m its predecessor is in S,
so a<c+m. A positive A-element is at least m+1, since m notin A; hence
its shifted level is>=1 and its upper level is<=q. A primitive p!=m must
be Apéry: otherwise p=m+(p-m) is a positive decomposition. Conversely the
notion of primitivity in A is the same as in S. The element m is primitive
and is outside A. Partitioning the m elements of A into0,e-1 primitives,
and delta decomposables gives m=e+delta, retaining right primitives.

Every positive S-element decomposes into primitives by induction on its
integer value. If a=x_1+...+x_t is Apéry and a positive partial sum v had
v-m in S, then a-m would be in S too, a contradiction. For a proper
partial sum the complement is>=m, so v<=a-m<c. Thus each primitive in a
decomposition of a decomposable A-value is left and belongs to B. This
argument concerns every representation and every partial sum. Adding a
positive S-element of size>=m raises lambda by at least1. A decomposable
value therefore has level>=2, proving kappa_1=0. Write a+rho=im+r,
0<=r<m. Its m-progression contributes q-i terms below c when i<q and0
when i=q; the zero progression contributes q. This proves the n formula
and q<=n. Substitute m=e+delta and c=qm-rho to obtain the last two
identities. Counting m together with B gives e>=1+s. A semigroup with a
single positive primitive is its multiples; cofiniteness would force m=1.
Thus e>=2 when S!=N. QED.

2. LEVEL BOUNDS, LENGTH, AND THE EMPTY BOUNDARY.
For x,y positive in S,
 lambda(x)+lambda(y)-1 <= lambda(x+y) <= lambda(x)+lambda(y)+1,
 lambda(x+y)>=lambda(x)+1, lambda(x+y)>=lambda(y)+1.       (2.1)
For a primitive-factor pattern l=(l_1,...,l_t), every represented value
has level in
 [max(max_i l_i+t-1, sum_i l_i-t+1), sum_i l_i+t-1].       (2.2)
An Apéry representation has t<=q; a target of level<=Q has t<=Q and
each l_i<=Q-t+1. If B is empty, D is empty. If n=q then T is empty,
D is empty, and c=qe-rho<=ne, independently of all other gates.

Proof. Write x+rho=im+r_x, y+rho=jm+r_y with0<=r_x,r_y<m.
The remaining floor is floor((r_x+r_y-rho)/m), between-1 and1.
The strict increments follow from x,y>=m. Iterate the floor inequalities
and, separately, add t-1 factors to a factor of largest level. Primitive
factors have level>=1, proving t<=Q and the indicated individual range.
The B-empty conclusion follows from Lemma1. If n=q all nonnegative
weighted alpha terms vanish, so T is empty and the same conclusion applies.
This also handles q=1: its T is empty. QED.

3. ELIGIBLE PAIRS, WEIGHTS, AND LOOP CONVENTION.
For a vector z=(z_1,...,z_(Q-1)) define
 P_Q(z)=sum_(i<j,i+j<=Q+1) z_i z_j
          +sum_(2i<=Q+1) binom(z_i+1,2),
 U_Q(z)=sum_(i<j,i+j<=Q) z_i z_j
          +sum_(2i<=Q) binom(z_i+1,2).                 (3.1)
Indices are1,...,Q-1; absent coordinates are0. These count unordered
pairs with replacement. Set M=P_q(alpha), U=U_q(alpha). Then delta<=M.
For q>=2 give each T-vertex weight w(x)=q-lambda(x), of total n-q.
Eligibility means w(x)+w(y)>=q-1. If n-q<q-1, no distinct-vertex pair
is eligible and M<=floor((n-q)/ceil((q-1)/2)). If2(n-q)<q-1, M=0.

Proof. Each D-value splits into two proper positive factors in T by
Lemma1. The lower floor bound makes their level sum<=q+1. Choosing one
pair per distinct value is injective into eligible formal pairs, since
a fixed pair has one numerical sum. Counting distinct-level and same-level
pairs gives(3.1). The weighted identity gives the total weight. Distinct
vertices have combined weight<=n-q, while an eligible loop must use a
vertex of weight>=ceil((q-1)/2). Any pair weight is<=2(n-q). QED.

4. ACTUAL SUM GRAPH, MISSING STARS, AND COLLISIONS.
Let E_g be the unordered pairs with replacement from T whose sum lies in A.
For a vertex u count its neighbors, a loop once, as deg(u). If u in R,
then deg(u)<=K. If such u has deg(u)>=2, then delta<=|E_g|-1.
If it has r eligible neighbors and a actual neighbors, |E_g|<=M-r+a.
Thus an occupied decomposable vertex with at least two eligible neighbors
implies delta<=M-1. Multiple missing-star losses may be added ONLY when
the sets of eligible pair objects in those stars are disjoint. For q>=4,
 delta<=M-kappa_2*alpha_(q-1).                           (4.1)

Proof. Write u=x+y with positive factors. For any actual neighbor v of u,
x+v is a proper left decomposable subsum of u+v, so v->x+v injects its
neighbor set into R. Suppose the sum map E_g->D were injective. The
equal-sum actual edges {v+x,y} and {v+y,x}, obtained by subsum closure,
must coincide. Since v>0, this forces x=y. Then the equal-sum edges
{v,u} and {x,v+x} must coincide, which forces v=x (the other pairing
would force x=0). There could then be only one neighbor. Thus two
neighbors force a collision and at least one lost image. The r-a missing
pairs in a single star are distinct even with a loop; disjoint star
sets permit their losses to add. If r>=2 and a<r there is a missing
edge; otherwise a>=2 gives the collision loss. Losses from alternative
arguments are intersected by a minimum, not added without disjointness.
For(4.1), any u in R_2 splits into two A_1 elements by strict increments.
If v in A_(q-1) and u+v were Apéry, one of those A_1 factors plus v
would be a proper left subsum of level>=q, impossible. All kappa_2*
alpha_(q-1) pairs are eligible and distinct; levels2 and q-1 differ at
q>=4. Hence they are missing. QED.

5. ONE GLOBAL COMPRESSION ALLOWANCE.
Let Cmp be the set of D-values z possessing ANY split z=x+y into
positive S-elements with lambda(z)=lambda(x)+lambda(y)-1. All such
factors are proper left Apéry values. Then |Cmp|<=rho. For every z
outside Cmp, EVERY such split satisfies lambda(z)>=lambda(x)+lambda(y).
One has delta<=U+rho. If delta<=F and |D\Cmp|<=N are separately proved,
 qdelta-rho <= min(qF,(q-1)F+U,(q-1)F+N).              (5.1)

Proof. For a compressed split with levels i,j, factor intervals give
(i+j)m<=z+2rho, while its sum level gives z+2rho<(i+j)m+rho.
Thus (z+2rho) mod m is in[0,rho)_Z. Distinct A-values have distinct
residues modulo m, and the same shift2rho preserves distinctness. This
is one injection for the union of ALL compressed values, regardless of
factor degree or level type. At rho=0 the set is empty. Integrality and
the lower floor bound give the universal noncompression assertion.
For a D-value outside the image of pairs of level sum<=q, any chosen
split has level sum q+1, hence its target level q is compressed. There
are at most rho such values and at most U values in the other image.
Finally delta-rho<=U and delta-rho<=N; combine each with
(q-1)delta<=(q-1)F. QED.

6. STRICT INTEGER ENDPOINTS AND CUTOFF BRANCHES.
Assume q<n, delta<=F and qdelta-rho<=sigma. Put
 e_min=max(2,1+s), E=floor((sigma-1)/(n-q)), C=qE+sigma.   (6.1)
Every strict violation has e_min<=e<=E and c<=C. After an independently
justified e<=3 Wilf dispatch, replace e_min by max(4,1+s). If
sigma<=(n-q)e_min or E<e_min, no strict violation exists. For any
integer H>=0 with a Wilf theorem for c<=H, remaining violations satisfy
c>=H+1; C<=H closes the branch. The genus<=G leaf closes c-n<=G.

Proof. All quantities are integers. From(n-q)e<qdelta-rho<=sigma one
gets(n-q)e<=sigma-1, proving E, with its indispensable subtraction1.
Since c=qe+(qdelta-rho), the conductor bound follows. Lemma1 supplies
the lower bound and counts full e; there is no equality e=1+s premise.
The other tests are direct inequalities and the stated imported leaves.
The q=n case uses Lemma2 rather than dividing by zero. QED.

7. CLASSICAL GROWTH AND THE COMPLETE BINOMIAL CONVENTION.
For j>=1 and a nonnegative integer a, use the canonical full j-binomial
expansion a=sum_(i=1)^j binom(a_i,i), with
a_j>...>a_1>=0, a_i>=i-1; zero tails have a_i=i-1. Define
 G_j(a)=sum_(i=1)^j binom(a_i+1,i+1), G_j(0)=0,
 H_(d,t)=G_(t-1) o ... o G_d for1<=d<t.                (7.1)
For0<=a<j, G_j(a)=a. These maps are increasing. The imported Macaulay
theorem says that the Hilbert dimensions of a standard graded
commutative Q-algebra obey h_(j+1)<=G_j(h_j); consequently
h_t<=H_(d,t)(h_d). Zero and small dimensions are included.

Proof of the convention and monotonicity. Greedily choose the largest
a_j with binom(a_j,j)<=a. Pascal's identity bounds the remainder by
binom(a_j,j-1), strictly, which forces the next positive top index
below a_j. Iterate, filling a zero tail with i-1. This proves existence
and uniqueness by induction. At a=0 all binomials vanish. For a<j the
a positive terms are leading unit binomials and growth leaves them1.
For the successor choose the smallest r with a_r+1<a_(r+1), taking
a_(j+1)=infinity. The lower indices through r are consecutive. Increase
a_r by1 and reset the indices below r to i-1. The hockey-stick identity
shows that this increases the original sum by exactly1 and the growth
sum by a_r-r+2>=1. The zero-tail endpoint gives the same identity
directly. Thus G_j is strictly increasing on nonnegative integers,
and compositions are increasing. Iterated Macaulay gives(7.1).
The growth theorem itself is the cited classical leaf; no shifted
Apéry level is used as a grading. QED.

8. FIXED-DEGREE NUMERICAL SHADOW ADAPTER (UNIVERSAL REPRESENTATIONS).
Let B0 be a finite set of DISTINCT integers (signs arbitrary), d<t
positive integers, K0 subset dB0 of cardinality a, and V subset tB0.
Suppose EVERY degree-t representation of EVERY v in V has EVERY
degree-d partial sum in K0. Then |V|<=H_(d,t)(a).

Proof. If B0 is empty V is empty. Otherwise enumerate b_0,...,b_(s-1),
attach variables X_i and v(X^u)=sum u_i b_i. In degree<=t use the
additive rank w(u)=sum u_i(t+1)^i. Each exponent is<=t, so ranks are
distinct in those degrees. Choose the least-rank degree-d representative
of each K0-value, obtaining a distinct monomial set Z_d of size a.
Choose the least-rank degree-t representative of each V-value. Each
degree-d divisor has its value in K0 by the UNIVERSAL hypothesis. If
it were not the selected representative, replacing it by the lower-rank
equal-value degree-d monomial would keep total degree t and numerical
value and decrease rank, a contradiction. In Q[X_0,...,X_(s-1)] kill
all degree-d monomials outside Z_d by a homogeneous monomial ideal.
The surviving degree-d basis has size a. A degree-t monomial survives
exactly when all its degree-d divisors survive. The chosen V-monomials
therefore inject into its degree-t basis. The quotient is generated
in degree1 over Q, with genuine polynomial total degree; Lemma7 yields
the claimed bound. Colliding numerical values cause no problem, and
the hypothesis is not replaced by a single chosen representation. QED.

9. UNIVERSAL NUMERICAL PREFIX SHADOWS.
For2<=Q<=q let B_Q consist of primitive left values of level<Q. If it
is empty, no decomposable A-value through level Q exists. Otherwise let
ell be its smallest occupied level. For t=3,...,Q and2<=d<t put
 L_d=max(ell+d-1,d(ell-1)+1),
 a_(Q,t,d)=sum_(L_d<=i<=Q-t+d) kappa_i.
Then, with empty ranges0,
 |D intersect union_(i<=Q) A_i|
 <=P_Q(beta)+sum_(t=3)^Q min_(2<=d<t) H_(d,t)(a_(Q,t,d)). (9.1)
For Q<q this bounds sum_(i<=Q)kappa_i; for Q=q it bounds delta.

Proof. Primitive factorizations of a target through Q use B_Q and have
length<=Q. Pair targets inject into P_Q(beta). Fix all numerical targets
having a length-t representation. For EVERY such representation and
EVERY degree-d partial sum, Lemmas1--2 give a proper decomposable left
Apéry value, lower level L_d, and upper level Q-t+d. Taking K0 to be
the intersection of dB_Q with that R-level range supplies Lemma8's
universal hypothesis. Its size is at most a_(Q,t,d); Lemma7's
monotonicity gives each bound. Take their minimum and then a union
bound over factor lengths. A value may have several lengths, so this
sum is an upper bound, never an assertion of disjoint numerical classes.
QED.

10. ALL-BIPARTITION OCCUPIED-LEVEL RECURSION.
Work with a primitive level multiset l. Singleton levels with beta_i=0
are unavailable; otherwise mu((i))=i. For length t>=2 examine EVERY
bipartition into nonempty submultisets v,w. If any proper piece is
unavailable, full-target bounds are unavailable. Otherwise define
 a(l)=max_(v+w=l){mu(v)+mu(w)-1,mu(v)+1,mu(w)+1},
 b(l)=max_(v+w=l){mu(v)+mu(w),mu(v)+1,mu(w)+1}.          (10.1)
For a PROPER nonsingleton sum, define mu(l) as the least j with
kappa_j>0 and
 a(l)<=j<=min(q-1,sum l_i+t-1);                        (10.2)
if no such j exists it is unavailable. This recursion is well founded.
Every proper actual sum with pattern l has level>=mu(l). A full target
through Q requires a(l)<=Q; a noncompressed full target requires
b(l)<=Q. Full targets are not rounded to left occupancy.

Proof. Both pieces have smaller length. By induction their actual levels
are at least the computed mu. Every split exists as an actual proper
subsum of a full Apéry representation. Lemma2 gives both floor and
strict-increment lower bounds, hence all entries of a(l). A proper
nonsingleton sum belongs to R, so its actual occupied level lies in
(10.2); the upper endpoint follows from Lemma2 and proper leftness.
Its level is therefore at least the least such j. Unavailability proves
that the represented proper sum cannot exist. At a noncompressed full
target, Lemma5's assertion for EVERY split supplies the extra floor
unit in b(l). The target may be in A_q, explaining why no left rounding
is imposed on the full target. Repeated levels and factors do not alter
this induction. QED.

11. FORMAL COUNTS, OCCUPIED SHADOW ENDPOINTS, AND NONCOMPRESSION.
For t=2,...,Q, enumerate ALL weakly increasing patterns of length t
with1<=l_i<=Q-t+1 and
max(max l_i+t-1,sum l_i-t+1)<=Q. If r_i is the multiplicity of level i,
its formal monomial count is product_i binom(beta_i+r_i-1,r_i), with
an r_i=0 factor1 and an r_i>0,beta_i=0 factor0. Retain available a(l)<=Q
for ordinary targets, available b(l)<=Q for noncompressed targets.
Call the totals A_(Q,t), B_(Q,t). These bound, respectively, numerical
targets with such a primitive representation, and their noncompressed
subfamily. For exact level j, also require sum l_i+t-1>=j and a(l)<=j
(or b(l)<=j). For Q<q all nonsingleton target levels must be occupied;
requiring some occupied j in the permitted range is another sound filter.

For d>=1 define a_d(Q) as follows. For d=1 take the least occupied
primitive level below Q. For d>=2 enumerate the same primitive patterns
at target bound Q-1, with nonzero multiplicity and available mu(l)<=Q-1,
and take the least mu(l). If the set is empty use sentinel Q. Every
degree-d proper sum of a target through Q has level>=a_d(Q). For2<=d<t
its level range is
 a_d(Q)<=j<=min(Q-t+d,Q-a_(t-d)(Q)+1),                 (11.1)
with the last +1 deleted for a noncompressed target. Intersect numerical
degree-t caps with H_(d,t)(sum kappa_j over this range), for EVERY d.
Empty ranges give0. Summing these valid per-length bounds gives ordinary
F_Q and noncompressed N_Q; overlap between lengths only enlarges the sum.

Proof. Lemma2 contains every actual factor pattern in the listed finite
domain. Stars and bars counts unordered choices with repetition from
beta_i distinct variables in each level. Choose a representative for
each distinct numerical value; a fixed monomial has a fixed value, so
this is an injection into retained monomials. Lemma10 justifies the
filters, including every split. Every proper d-subsum has level<=Q-1,
so its pattern appears in the a_d enumeration and gives the asserted
lower bound. For a complementary subsum of t-d factors the lower bound
is a_(t-d); the ordinary floor inequality yields the second upper

12. FORCED COMPRESSED LEFT-PAIR SUBTRACTION.
Suppose a collection J of layers j<q is proved pair-only: for each such
j, every primitive pattern of every length3<=t<=j fails the exact-level
ordinary filters of Lemma11 (possibly with a valid cutoff or rank filter).
Let p_j count ALL formal primitive pairs capable of a noncompressed
target at exact level j by Lemma11, and put f_j=max(0,kappa_j-p_j).
Then, provided this profile is actual,
 number of noncompressed D-values having a primitive pair representation
 <=U_q(beta)-sum_(j in J) f_j.                         (12.1)
A negative right side proves the profile impossible; it is not a negative
cardinality bound applied to a surviving object.

Proof. Length>j is impossible by Lemma2; the hypothesis excludes the
other longer lengths, so every R_j value has a primitive pair
representation. Such a pair has level sum<=j+1<=q and is counted in
U_q(beta). At most p_j of the kappa_j values are noncompressed, so
choose f_j distinct compressed values and one primitive pair for each.
Distinct numerical values, also across different layers, give distinct
pair representatives. No noncompressed value can use one of these pairs,
because its numerical sum would be the chosen compressed value. Selecting
a pair for each distinct noncompressed pair-value injects that family into
the remaining U_q(beta) pairs. If the number to remove exceeds U_q(beta),
even the required chosen compressed representatives cannot exist. QED.

13. ONE GLOBAL DEGREE-FIRST, RANK-SECOND ORDER IDEAL.
Enumerate B=b_0,...,b_(s-1). For EVERY B-representable Apéry value,
including0, select the monomial minimizing lexicographically
 (|u|,w(u)), w(u)=sum u_i(q+1)^i.                     (13.1)
There is a unique selection. Its set Z is divisor closed, and its
numerical map is injective globally, including across degrees. Z consists
of1, the s primitive variables, and one monomial for each D-value.
The complement of Z is a monomial ideal in Q[X_0,...,X_(s-1)]; its
quotient is standard graded by TRUE TOTAL DEGREE, with basis Z.
For2<=d<=q-1,1<=j<q define
 h[d,j]=#{u in Z:|u|=d, v(u) in R_j}.
Then h[d,j]>=0, h[d,j]=0 for d>j, and
 sum_(d=2)^(q-1)h[d,j]=kappa_j.                       (13.2)
This SAME allocation is used throughout all prefixes, degrees and shadows.

Proof. All representations have degree<=q by Lemma2, so there are
finitely many and their base(q+1) ranks are distinct. For a divisor v
of a selected u, its value is Apéry by Lemma1. If its selected equal-value
representative v' has smaller degree, replacement u-v+v' decreases total
degree; if degrees agree it decreases additive rank. Either contradicts
(13.1). Thus every divisor is selected. A divisor-closed monomial set has
upward-closed complement; that complement generates the claimed homogeneous
monomial ideal. Degree0 is Q and degree1 variables generate the quotient.
There is exactly one selected representative for each represented value;
right primitives are not B-representable, whereas all D-values are, by
Lemma1. This proves the stated partition and global injection, resolving
cross-degree numerical collisions. A left value at level j cannot have
degree>j, so(13.2) follows. For B empty the ring is Q, Z={1}, D empty.
QED.

14. SELECTED-DIVISOR SHADOWS, SHARED CAPS, AND THE EXACT TOP SPLIT.
Fix Q<=q,2<=t<=Q, and a subset Y of selected degree-t targets through Q.

use the formal count. Then
 sum_(j<=Q)h[t,j]<=C_(Q,t)(h) for Q<q,
 delta<=F_h=sum_(t=2)^q C_(q,t)(h),
 |D\Cmp|<=N_h=sum_(t=2)^q Cnc_(q,t)(h).               (14.1)
For a degree t write L_t=sum_(j<q)h[t,j], interpreted0 for t=q.
Every selected degree-t value consists of exactly L_t left values plus

24. JOINT INTEGER-POINT MAXIMUM MATCHING.

with earlier greedy decisions. If the current interval has no unused
point, earlier fixed matches occupy all its points, so it must be
skipped. Otherwise let x be its smallest unused point. If the maximum
assigns it y, then y>=x. If x is free, replace y by x; if x is assigned
